# Untold — Product & Design Specification

## 1. Product Overview

**Untold (Everyone has an untold story.)** is an AI-assisted storytelling
platform that helps people turn their memories, experiences, ideas, imagination,
and unfinished thoughts into meaningful stories.

The central idea behind Untold is:

> **Everyone has a story. Not everyone knows how to tell it.**

Users can start with something as simple as a few notes, a memory, an image, a
topic, or an idea. Untold helps them develop that raw material into a
structured, engaging, long-form story.

The AI should feel like a **creative writing companion**, not a replacement for
the writer.

The user remains the author.

AI helps them:

- Find the story inside their ideas
- Develop their ideas
- Organize their thoughts
- Create story structures
- Write chapters
- Continue unfinished stories
- Improve existing writing
- Brainstorm possibilities
- Develop characters
- Improve pacing and emotional impact

The experience should feel personal, creative, calm, and inspiring.

---

# 2. Product Positioning

Untold should not feel like:

> "An AI text generator."

It should feel like:

> **"A place where your stories become real."**

The product should help someone go from:

> "I have this memory but I don't know how to write about it."

to:

> "I have a story."

The user should feel that they are **creating something**, rather than filling
out an AI prompt form.

---

# 3. Core Product Principles

### The user is the author

AI should assist the user rather than take ownership of their story.

### Ideas don't need to be polished

Users should be able to enter:

- Random thoughts
- Fragments
- Memories
- Bullet points
- Conversations
- Events
- Emotions
- Images
- Rough descriptions

The application should make imperfect input feel welcome.

### Stories grow over time

A story should not be treated as a single AI generation.

Stories can evolve over days, weeks, or months.

### AI should be contextual

AI assistance should appear where it is useful rather than overwhelming the
interface.

### The interface should inspire writing

The UI should feel closer to a **beautiful writing studio** than a traditional
SaaS dashboard.

---

# 4. Primary User Journey

The primary experience should be:

**Idea → Explore → Shape → Write → Continue → Refine → Share**

For example:

1. User opens Untold.
2. User starts a story.
3. They write a few rough notes.
4. They optionally add an image.
5. They choose a story type or topic.
6. Untold helps them understand what their story could become.
7. AI proposes possible directions.
8. User chooses the direction they like.
9. The first chapter is created.
10. User edits and adds their own writing.
11. AI helps when they get stuck.
12. User chooses **Continue Story**.
13. The next chapter develops naturally.
14. The process continues until the story is complete.
15. User shares the finished story.

---

# 5. Story Creation

Creating a story should feel approachable.

Do not begin with a large form containing many required fields.

The first question should be something simple and inviting:

> **What story do you want to tell?**

Supporting copy could say:

> It doesn't need to be perfect. Start with a memory, an idea, a moment, or
> anything that's been on your mind.

The user can immediately start writing.

Additional information should be optional.

---

# 6. Story Information

A story can contain:

- Title
- Description
- Notes
- Topic
- Category
- Story type
- Additional AI instructions
- Cover image
- Tags
- Chapters
- Story status
- Visibility

These should not all be presented at once.

The interface should reveal complexity progressively.

---

# 7. Story Types

Users can optionally choose the type of story they are creating.

Examples:

- Personal Story
- Memoir
- Life Experience
- Fiction
- Short Story
- Novel
- Romance
- Mystery
- Fantasy
- Adventure
- Horror
- Historical
- Inspirational
- Children's Story

The AI should use the selected type to influence its suggestions and writing
style.

---

# 8. Topics

Topics help users communicate what their story is about.

Examples:

- Childhood
- Family
- Friendship
- Love
- Loss
- Adventure
- Growing Up
- Dreams
- Travel
- Identity
- Hope
- Fear
- Life Changes

Topics should feel like helpful inspiration rather than mandatory metadata.

---

# 9. Notes

Notes are one of the most important parts of Untold.

Users should be encouraged to write freely.

For example:

> I remember going to my grandfather's house every summer. There was an old tree
> behind the house. One summer I found something buried underneath it.

This doesn't need to be good writing.

Untold should help turn it into something meaningful.

Potential AI actions:

- **Find the story**
- **Expand this**
- **What could happen next?**
- **Turn this into a story**
- **Make it more emotional**
- **Give me some directions**

---

# 10. Images

Users can optionally attach images to their stories.

Images can represent:

- Memories
- People
- Places
- Objects
- Inspiration
- Characters
- Story settings

Images should feel like part of the creative process rather than a generic
file-upload feature.

For example, the story creation experience could visually present:

> **Add something that inspires this story**

rather than simply:

> Upload file.

---

# 11. AI Story Development

AI should help the user discover the potential of their story before immediately
writing a huge amount of text.

For example, after entering notes, Untold could present:

### Your story could become...

**A nostalgic coming-of-age story**

A story about a child discovering something hidden beneath their grandfather's
old tree, eventually uncovering a family secret.

Then offer several directions:

**Make it mysterious**

**Make it emotional**

**Keep it close to real life**

**Surprise me**

This makes AI feel like a creative partner.

---

# 12. Story Direction

When the user's idea is ambiguous, AI should suggest possibilities instead of
making one irreversible decision.

For example:

### Where could this story go?

**A — The Family Secret**

The discovery reveals something your grandfather never told anyone.

**B — A Childhood Adventure**

The discovery begins an adventure that changes how you remember your childhood.

**C — Keep It Real**

Build this around the emotions and memories you actually experienced.

The user chooses the direction.

This keeps the user in control.

---

# 13. Story Outline

Before generating a long story, Untold should optionally help create an outline.

The outline could contain:

- Story premise
- Main characters
- Setting
- Conflict
- Major events
- Emotional arc
- Chapter structure
- Possible ending

The user should be able to modify the outline.

AI suggestions should never feel locked.

---

# 14. Chapters

Long stories should be divided into chapters or sections.

Example:

**Chapter 01 — The House Behind the Hill**

**Chapter 02 — The Summer We Found It**

**Chapter 03 — What Was Buried**

**Chapter 04 — The Letter**

**Chapter 05 — The Truth**

The user should be able to:

- Add chapters
- Rename chapters
- Reorder chapters
- Edit chapters
- Delete chapters
- Ask AI for help
- Continue the story

---

# 15. "To Be Continued"

This is a signature Untold experience.

At the end of a chapter, the user should encounter something more inspiring than
a generic "Add Chapter" button.

For example:

> **There's more to this story.**

**Continue it**

or:

> **To be continued...**

This should create a natural transition into the next chapter.

The AI should understand what happened previously and help continue the story
while preserving continuity.

---

# 16. Getting Unstuck

A major product problem Untold should solve is:

> "I don't know what happens next."

The user should be able to ask:

> **I'm stuck. Help me.**

AI can then suggest:

- Three possible directions
- A surprising twist
- A character decision
- A conflict
- An emotional moment
- A new discovery
- A transition into the next chapter

The user chooses what inspires them.

---

# 17. AI Writing Assistance

AI tools should be contextual.

When selecting text, useful actions might include:

- Improve
- Expand
- Simplify
- Make it more emotional
- Make it more descriptive
- Rewrite
- Change tone
- Improve dialogue

When working at the chapter level:

- Continue
- Summarize
- Find inconsistencies
- Suggest improvements
- Brainstorm what happens next

When working at the story level:

- Improve the plot
- Suggest characters
- Suggest conflicts
- Suggest an ending
- Review story structure

---

# 18. AI Should Not Take Over

Avoid making the primary interaction:

> **Generate Entire Story**

as the dominant action.

That may exist, but it should not define the product.

The experience should encourage:

> **You + AI**

rather than:

> **AI writes everything for you.**

---

# 19. Personal Stories

Untold should be especially good for people telling real-life stories.

Examples:

- Childhood memories
- Family stories
- Relationships
- Travel experiences
- Life-changing moments
- Stories about parents or grandparents
- Personal achievements
- Difficult experiences
- Funny memories
- Stories they want to preserve for their children

For real-life stories, AI should help with storytelling without casually
inventing facts.

The user's memories remain the source of truth.

---

# 20. Fiction

Untold should also work for fictional storytelling.

AI can help with:

- Characters
- Worlds
- Settings
- Plot
- Conflict
- Dialogue
- Twists
- Themes
- Endings
- Subplots

The experience should be equally useful for someone writing a novel from
scratch.

---

# 21. Story Workspace

The story workspace is the heart of the product.

It should feel like entering a **private creative studio**.

The user should have easy access to:

- Story information
- Chapters
- Writing area
- AI assistance
- Notes
- Story context
- Images
- Story settings

Avoid making the workspace look like a database administration panel.

---

# 22. Visual Design Direction

Visual quality is a **core product requirement**, not a finishing step.

The interface should be:

- Elegant
- Editorial
- Warm
- Modern
- Immersive
- Minimal
- Human
- Comfortable for long reading and writing sessions

Avoid generic AI SaaS aesthetics.

---

# 23. Avoid "AI Slop"

The application must deliberately avoid common AI-generated interface patterns.

Do NOT default to:

- Excessive gradients
- Giant glowing AI buttons
- Purple/blue gradient backgrounds
- Excessive glassmorphism
- Generic rounded cards everywhere
- Excessive shadows
- Dashboard-card overload
- Emoji as UI decoration
- Generic "AI ✨" labels everywhere
- Huge hero sections with meaningless marketing copy
- Excessive pills
- Overly dense sidebars
- Identical card grids
- Random decorative blobs
- Overuse of icons
- Every element inside a rounded rectangle

The interface should feel designed by a thoughtful product designer.

---

# 24. Editorial Design Inspiration

The visual language should take inspiration from:

- Beautiful digital magazines
- Literary publications
- Modern publishing platforms
- High-end writing applications
- Independent creative software
- Contemporary editorial websites
- Physical books
- Personal journals

Typography should be an important part of the identity.

The reading experience should feel particularly beautiful.

---

# 25. Typography

Typography should establish hierarchy and personality.

Consider using a combination of:

- A distinctive serif for story titles/headings
- A clean sans-serif for application UI
- A highly readable typeface for long-form story content

Typography should not simply default to the standard system font everywhere.

However, readability must always take priority over novelty.

---

# 26. Color Direction

The color system should feel sophisticated and literary.

Avoid the standard:

> white + purple gradient + blue accent

AI SaaS aesthetic.

Instead, consider a restrained palette built around:

- Warm neutrals
- Off-white
- Ink-like dark tones
- Subtle warm accent colors
- One distinctive brand accent

Dark mode should also be considered.

---

# 27. Motion

Animations should be subtle and purposeful.

Good uses:

- Story opening
- Chapter transitions
- AI generation
- Saving
- Toast notifications
- Panel transitions
- Text appearing during generation

Avoid:

- Constant floating animations
- Excessive bouncing
- Decorative motion
- Slow animations that make the interface feel sluggish

Motion should reinforce the feeling of a living creative workspace.

---

# 28. Empty States

Empty states should inspire rather than look like errors.

Instead of:

> No stories found.

Prefer:

> **Your next story hasn't been written yet.**

Then:

> Start with a memory, an idea, or even a single sentence.

**Start a story**

---

# 29. Dashboard

The dashboard should not be a generic analytics dashboard.

The primary focus should be the user's stories.

Potential structure:

### Good morning.

> What story are you thinking about today?

Then:

**Continue writing**

Recent stories should appear as visual story previews rather than generic data
cards.

For example:

> **The Summer We Never Forgot** Chapter 4 · Edited yesterday

> **Letters to My Father** Draft · 2 chapters

> **The House at the End of the Road** Draft · 7 chapters

---

# 30. Story Cards

Story cards should feel closer to book covers or editorial previews.

They can contain:

- Cover
- Title
- Short excerpt
- Chapter count
- Last edited date
- Status

Avoid turning them into overly complicated UI cards.

---

# 31. Reading Experience

The public story reader should be one of the most beautiful parts of Untold.

It should feel like reading a digital book.

Focus on:

- Excellent typography
- Comfortable line length
- Generous whitespace
- Beautiful chapter transitions
- Minimal distractions

The reader should not feel like they are reading content inside a CRUD
application.

---

# 32. Public Story Page

A public story could begin with:

**The Summer We Never Forgot**

_A story about childhood, memory, and the things we leave behind._

**Written by [Author]**

Then transition into:

> Chapter One The House Behind the Hill

The story content should occupy the visual center of the experience.

---

# 33. Sharing

Sharing should feel meaningful.

After completing a story:

> **Your story is ready to be shared.**

Then provide:

**Share privately**

**Share with a link**

**Publish publicly**

The user should always understand who can see the story.

---

# 34. Story Privacy

Privacy is especially important because users may write deeply personal stories.

Default behavior should be private.

Clearly communicate:

- Who can see the story
- Whether the story is public
- Whether the link is accessible
- Whether attached images are visible

Avoid ambiguous privacy controls.

---

# 35. Navigation

Navigation should remain simple.

Possible primary navigation:

- Home
- My Stories
- Discover
- Shared
- Settings

The exact structure can evolve.

Do not add navigation items simply because the underlying database has another
entity.

Navigation should represent user goals.

---

# 36. Design With Impeccable

**Impeccable should be treated as part of the design workflow.**

When improving the UI, use Impeccable to critique and refine the interface
rather than simply asking AI to "make it prettier."

The goal is to identify:

- Generic UI patterns
- Weak hierarchy
- Poor typography
- Excessive visual noise
- Inconsistent spacing
- Poor information density
- Weak empty states
- Unnecessary decoration
- Generic AI aesthetics
- Accessibility issues
- Poor responsive behavior

Design iterations should be driven by specific observations.

---

# 37. Impeccable Design Principle

Do not ask:

> "Make this UI look better."

Instead ask questions such as:

> "Make this feel like a premium literary writing application rather than a
> generic SaaS dashboard."

or:

> "Improve the hierarchy and typography while reducing unnecessary cards and
> visual noise."

or:

> "Identify and remove AI-generated design patterns from this interface."

or:

> "Make the story reading experience feel closer to a beautifully designed
> digital book."

This produces more meaningful design improvements.

---

# 38. Responsive Design

Untold must work well across:

- Desktop
- Laptop
- Tablet
- Mobile

Desktop should provide the richest writing experience.

Mobile should prioritize:

- Reading
- Quick notes
- Editing
- AI assistance
- Continuing stories

Do not simply shrink the desktop interface.

Design mobile layouts intentionally.

# 40. Development Quality Bar

Every feature should be evaluated from three perspectives:

### Product

Does this actually help someone create or enjoy a story?

### UX

Is the interaction intuitive and enjoyable?

### Visual design

Does it feel like a polished product rather than generated boilerplate?

A feature is not considered complete simply because it technically works.

---

# 41. Claude's Role

When working on Untold, Claude should behave as a **senior product engineer
working alongside a product designer**.

Before implementing a feature:

1. Understand the user's goal.
2. Inspect the existing application.
3. Reuse existing functionality where possible.
4. Avoid unnecessary complexity.
5. Consider the complete user flow.
6. Consider loading, empty, error, and success states.
7. Consider mobile behavior.
8. Consider visual hierarchy.
9. Ensure the feature feels consistent with Untold.
10. Test the result before considering it complete.

---

# 42. Design Before Implementation

For significant UI work, think about the experience before writing components.

Ask:

- What is the user's goal?
- What should they notice first?
- What should they do next?
- What information is actually necessary?
- What can be hidden until needed?
- What should feel delightful?
- What could make the experience feel generic?
- How does this look on mobile?
- Does this feel like Untold?

Do not add UI merely because a feature exists technically.

---

# 43. Avoid Overengineering

The product should remain simple for the user even if the underlying application
becomes sophisticated.

Do not expose technical concepts such as:

- AI context
- Model configuration
- Prompt construction
- Token usage
- AI memory
- Generation parameters

unless these become intentional advanced features later.

The user should think about:

> **My story**

not:

> **How the AI system works.**

---

# 44. The Emotional Goal

The most important question for every major feature is:

> **Does this make someone more excited to tell their story?**

A successful Untold experience should make users feel:

- "I want to keep writing."
- "I didn't know my idea could become this."
- "This helped me express something I've wanted to say."
- "I want to see what happens next."
- "This feels like my story."

---

# 45. Brand Statement

The product can be positioned around:

> **Everyone has a story.**

And the emotional continuation:

> **Untold helps you tell it.**

Alternative:

> **Your story is already there. We'll help you find it.**

Another:

> **From a thought to a story.**

---

# 46. Ultimate Product Vision

Untold should eventually become more than an AI writing tool.

It should become a **home for people's stories**.

A place where someone can:

- Capture a memory
- Start an idea
- Develop a fictional world
- Write about their life
- Preserve family stories
- Collaborate with AI
- Continue writing over time
- Publish their work
- Discover stories from other people

The long-term vision is:

> **A world where everyone feels capable of becoming a storyteller.**

---

## Final instruction

**Do not treat the existing boilerplate as something that needs to be redesigned
or replaced unnecessarily.**

The existing project already provides the technical foundation.

The focus from this point forward is:

**Product → User Experience → Visual Design → Storytelling → AI Collaboration →
Quality**

Use the existing project structure and technology as the foundation, and
concentrate development effort on turning it into a polished, production-quality
storytelling product.

**Do not settle for generic AI-generated SaaS UI.**

When a UI feels generic, overly rounded, overly colorful, overly animated,
excessively card-based, or visually similar to common AI products, reconsider
it.

Use **Impeccable** to critique and refine the design, with particular attention
to typography, hierarchy, spacing, composition, accessibility, responsiveness,
and removal of "AI slop."

The goal is not to make Untold look impressive because it contains many visual
effects.

The goal is to make it feel **intentional, beautiful, memorable, and worthy of
people's stories.**
